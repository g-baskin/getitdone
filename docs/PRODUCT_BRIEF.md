# Product brief: Get It Done

## The problem

When there are too many projects, too many half-finished things, and too many people you're waiting on, everything feels equally urgent. The result is being busy without moving forward, then feeling stuck and unmotivated.

Most to-do apps make this worse. They make it easy to add more and never ask you to choose.

## What the app does

It makes you choose. Every day it asks for **one thing** that, once it's done, makes the rest of the day easier or unnecessary. Everything else on the page supports that choice:

- what you're working on (only a few projects at a time)
- what's important today
- who and what is blocking you, and what you'll do about it
- what you're waiting on, and when to follow up

The page fits on one sheet of paper, so it can be printed, saved as a PDF, or kept open on a phone.

## Who it's for

One person: the owner. It's a personal tool, not a product for sale and not a team app.

## Principles

1. **One thing first.** The day's one thing is the biggest item on the page. There is only one.
2. **Choosing beats listing.** A short active list with clear "not now" decisions beats a long list of maybes.
3. **Blockers get a plan, not just a name.** Every blocker gets a "if this happens, then I'll do this" plan.
4. **Nothing gets lost.** Unsolved blockers and open waiting items carry over each day until they're dealt with.
5. **Proof builds confidence.** Wins are saved so you can look back at real evidence that you get things done.
6. **Energy is part of the work.** Sleep, movement and short resets count, because they decide how well everything else goes.
7. **No guilt.** No streaks or shaming. A bad day can be a "minimum day": just the one thing plus one win.
8. **Five minutes or less.** If planning the day takes longer than five minutes, the app is in the way.

## The routine

| When | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | about 5 minutes | Pick today's word and one thing, name who's counting on you, list the top 3 results, review blockers and waiting items |
| During the day | 50-minute blocks | Focus timer, then a 2-minute reset: breathe, stand up, and write what the next block is for |
| Evening | about 2 minutes | Did the one thing happen? Record one win and one lesson, and pencil in tomorrow's one thing |
| Sunday | about 15 minutes | Weekly review: what mattered, where the time went, what to park, and next week's focus |

## The daily page (one printed page)

```
SATURDAY, OCT 31                          Today's word: ____________

TODAY'S ONE THING  ______________________________________________  [ ]
  Why it matters: ______________     Who's counting on me: ____________

WORKING ON (max 3)                  | WHAT'S IMPORTANT TODAY (top 3)
 1. Project → next step             |  1.
 2.                                 |  2.
 3.                                 |  3.

BLOCKERS
 What / who | Type (person, decision, info, money, skill, fear) | If ___, then I'll ___

WAITING ON
 What | From whom | Since | Follow up on      (highlighted when overdue)

ENERGY   Sleep ___ h    Moved [ ]    Focus blocks  ○ ○ ○ ○

TONIGHT  Did the one thing? [ ]   Win: ____________   Lesson: ____________
         Tomorrow's one thing: ________________________________________
```

Blank fields print as blank lines, so an empty page can be filled in by hand.

## The weekly review (one printed page)

- **Who I want to be next week** in four areas: myself, the people around me, the skills I'm building, and the difference I'm making for others.
- **Ratings from 1 to 10** for clarity, energy, drive, output, impact on others, and courage, with an optional one-line note for each.
- **Where the time went:** an estimated % of the week spent on my main output (the work that actually moves my goal).
- **Parked projects:** keep parked, restart, or drop.
- **Top 3 wins** of the week.
- **Next week:** the main focus and the step on the goal it moves forward.

## Ideas behind the features

Each feature comes from an idea in one of the two books. The app uses its own wording for all of them.

| Idea | Source | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Doing the one task that makes the others easier or unnecessary | *The ONE Thing* | The required, single "Today's one thing" field, printed biggest |
| Being clear about who you want to be, and the feeling you bring | *High Performance Habits*: clarity | Today's word; the four-area intentions in the weekly review |
| Using breaks between tasks to reset, then setting an intention for the next one | *High Performance Habits*: energy | 50-minute focus timer with a 2-minute reset screen |
| Sleep, movement and food decide your capacity | *High Performance Habits*: energy | Energy line on the daily page |
| Knowing who is counting on you and saying why it matters | *High Performance Habits*: necessity | "Who's counting on me" and "Why it matters" lines |
| Focusing on the few outputs that matter and saying no to most other things | *High Performance Habits*: productivity | A limit of 3 active projects, a parked list that needs a reason, and the weekly "% on main output" check |
| Breaking a big goal into a handful of major steps | *High Performance Habits*: productivity | Goal plus up to 5 big steps; each day's one thing can link to one of them |
| Facing fear and treating struggle as part of the path | *High Performance Habits*: courage | "Fear" as a blocker type, which asks for the smallest brave step |
| Confidence grows from proven competence | *High Performance Habits*: confidence | The Proof Log, a running list of wins |
| Watching out for never feeling satisfied and for neglecting health and relationships | *High Performance Habits*: traps | No streaks, minimum-day mode, and health and relationship checks in the weekly review |
| Planning ahead for obstacles ("if X, then I'll do Y") | Goal-setting research cited in *High Performance Habits* | An if-then plan on every blocker |

## What it is not

- Not a full project manager, calendar, or team tool.
- Not a coaching program or therapy.
- Not a place to copy the books. It turns their ideas into a short daily routine, in original words.
